    Services opportunities · one-page summary

    Creating Services opportunitiesthrough customer outcomes

    The idea. Customers describe symptoms, not Services needs. Spot the symptom, ask one useful question, then hand off to the right owner. You create the opening, not the scope.

    Listen for four gaps

    ValueWe are not seeing the benefits we expected.
    AdoptionTeams are not really using it.
    RiskWe are behind and the date cannot move.
    CapacityWe do not have the people or expertise.

    Match it to one of four plays

    1Adoption and innovation

    When the customer is live, but value hasn’t landed.

    Ask Which capability is live but not yet delivering the value you expected?

    2OSM / managed operations

    When the team can’t run and evolve the platform.

    Ask Do you have the capacity and expertise to run and improve the platform after go-live?

    3Renewal value recovery

    When value evidence or sentiment is weak before renewal.

    Ask What would your sponsor need to see to feel confident about the renewal?

    4AI, WEM and digital acceleration

    When ambition is high, but readiness is unclear.

    Ask What business outcome should this improve, and what would prove it worked?

    Where to focus first

    Accounts where limited capacity, specialised expertise or delivery resources are holding back AI adoption and expansion.

    Also strong candidates: significant unused AI token capacity, or room to move up a Level of Experience Orchestration.

    Never say, say instead

    You need Services.This looks like something Services should help qualify.
    That will only take a few days.We should validate the scope before making commitments.
    We can include that in the current project.There may be a Services angle here, but we need to understand the outcome and urgency first.

    Never commit scope, effort, dates or price. Bring in the SED and the AE.

    01 · Recognise

    Opportunities don’t arrive labelled

    Most Services conversations start with one of four gaps. Use the gap as an entry point, then connect it to outcomes and timing. Select a gap to see what to ask next.

    →Spot the symptom early: reduce risk, protect value and involve the right people before pressure builds.
    Principle: spot it early, while it is still easy to fix. Customer Success is often the first to hear these symptoms. Recognised early, they help customers reduce risk and realise value faster. Missed, they return as escalations, renewal risk or stalled expansion. The same customer may show more than one gap.
    02 · Your role

    You create the opening, not the scope

    Your value is customer context and a warm hand-off. Qualification, scoping and commercials sit with others.

    What you should do

    • Spot signals
    • Ask discovery questions
    • Connect the issue to business outcomes
    • Introduce the Services angle
    • Make a warm hand-off
    • Stay involved as customer context owner

    What you shouldn’t do

    • Scope the work
    • Estimate effort
    • Commit dates or resources
    • Promise commercials
    • Own the sales cycle
    • Replace the AE or SED
    You · Customer Success

    Customer context owner

    Knows what the customer is trying to achieve, what is stuck, who is frustrated and what is at risk. Opens the conversation and stays involved.

    Account Executive

    Owns the commercial opportunity, including licensing and anything sold on a Sales Order.

    Services · SED

    Qualify, shape, scope, deliver

    Own qualification, shaping, scoping and delivery commitments for work sold on a Statement of Work.

    04 · The plays

    Focus on the plays you’re most likely to spot

    Four practical field patterns, not a full Services catalogue. Spot the play, ask one useful question, then route to the right owner.

    Wider map: from customer situation to Services angle

    Start with the customer situation. The Services offer comes later, so avoid naming a specific scope too early.

    Customer situationPossible Services angle
    New implementation, migration or rolloutImplementation services (use cases), delivery readiness, governance
    Poor adoption or stalled valueAdoption, optimisation, change readiness
    Limited internal skills or bandwidthOperational Service Management / managed operations
    AI, digital, WEM or automation ambitionDiscovery, roadmap, use case design, implementation, A2O
    Renewal risk or unclear ROIValue realisation, health check, improvement planning
    Large transformation programmeCX advisory, change management, governance

      05 · Discovery

      Ask questions that reveal outcome, risk and urgency

      Lightweight questions to judge whether this is a small adoption nudge, a support issue or a business problem worth involving Services. Select any question to copy it.

      Principle: discover lightly, route early. The goal is not to interrogate the customer. Listen for urgency and business impact, and hand the conversation on as soon as scope or delivery commitments come up.
      06 · Language

      Position Services without it sounding like a sales pitch

      Consultative language that opens the right conversation without making promises. Frame Services as a way to assess and shape the right path, not as the conclusion.

      Avoid accidental problems

      The safest language opens the door while protecting the customer and Genesys. Never make delivery, effort, resource or commercial commitments.

      08 · What counts

      What counts towards the Services number, and what doesn’t

      Only certain items contribute to Services revenue. Licensing does not count at all.

      Contributes directly

      • Any kind of labour (use cases), sold on a SoWLabour counts towards the Labour line only when a Genesys project entry exists.
      • Expert Apps, sold on a SoWFor example Iris, Blackchair.
      • Training (Education), sold on a SO or a SoW
      • Enablement sold on a SOFor example CX Cloud, A3S, R2S, CX as Code, Guidewire. These count because a project is generated even when sold on a SO.
      • Innovations licensing, sold on a SOFor example A3S, R2S, PS AppFoundry assets. Counts towards the Innovations line.

      Doesn’t contribute directly

      • Genesys Cloud licences
      • Third-party AppFoundry
      • Load and penetration testingOnly sold on a SO in any case.
      • Free-of-charge work that does not contribute to billable utilisationFor example IGC, free SoWs.
      Rule: if in doubt, ask a SED. AEs own the commercial motion for licensing and anything sold on a Sales Order; SEDs cannot raise Sales Orders. Services and SEDs own qualification, shaping, scoping and delivery commitments for SoWs.

      Cheat sheet

      When to think “Services”

      The customer has

      • Value · outcomes not proven, ROI unclear
      • Adoption · low usage or old ways of working
      • Risk · hard deadlines, unclear ownership
      • Capacity · small team, overloaded admins
      • Complexity · integrations or multi-region rollout
      • Innovation · AI, digital, WEM or automation ambition
      • Renewal · weak sentiment or value gap

      Questions to ask

      • What outcome are you trying to improve?
      • What is not working as expected today?
      • Where is the friction: people, process, platform, reporting or capacity?
      • What happens if this does not improve?
      • Why now?
      • Do you have the internal skills and bandwidth?
      • Who owns this internally?
      • What would success look like in 90 days?

      Four Services plays

      1. Adoption and innovation
      2. OSM / managed operations
      3. Renewal value recovery
      4. AI, WEM and digital acceleration
